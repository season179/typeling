# Episode 1 story shortlist

Stories chosen for a 7-year-old Malaysian Chinese reader with developing English.

Selection criteria: **short**, **simple vocabulary**, **repetition** (helps both reading and typing muscle memory), **concrete and visual**, and a **gentle moral**. All picks are public-domain, so the text can be freely rewritten to fit the chapter/typing format.

## Top pick — Aesop's Fables

~100–200 words each, plain vocabulary, clear moral. One fable = one episode, or split into 2–3 tiny chapters.

| Fable | Theme | Why it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Lion and the Mouse | Small kindness is repaid | Very short, two characters |
| The Tortoise and the Hare | Steady effort wins | Famous, easy to picture |
| The Crow and the Pitcher | Cleverness & not giving up | Concrete, visual |
| The Ant and the Grasshopper | Prepare for the future | Simple cause → effect |
| The North Wind and the Sun | Gentleness beats force | Lovely message |
| The Boy Who Cried Wolf | Honesty | Repetition built in |

## Local hook — Sang Kancil

Malaysian mouse-deer trickster tales (*Sang Kancil and the Crocodiles*, *…and the Tiger*). Short, witty, episodic, and **culturally familiar** — the personal connection makes them feel meaningful. Theme: brains over brawn.

## Best for typing repetition

Repeated refrains build keyboard muscle memory.

- **The Little Red Hen** — "Not I," said the cat / the dog / the pig. Theme: work earns reward.
- **Stone Soup** — sharing and community; repetitive "add one more thing" structure.
- **The Gingerbread Man** — "Run, run, as fast as you can!" repeats throughout.

## Worth a look (slightly longer, splittable)

- **The Magic Paintbrush (Ma Liang)** — a Chinese folk tale about kindness, fairness, and not being greedy. Familiar cultural roots; splits naturally into chapters.

## Episode 1 starting pool

Begin with **2–3 Aesop fables + 1 Sang Kancil tale**. Shortest, simplest, clearly meaningful, and all public-domain.

## Rules when adapting these into chapters

- British English spelling.
- Stay within `[A-Za-z0-9 .,!?'";:\-()\n]+` (violations are hard failures).
- Real names are allowed — e.g. the child's own name can be the hero. (There is no fictional-name requirement.)
- Keep chapters short enough for a single typing session.
